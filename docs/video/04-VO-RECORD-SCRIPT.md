# VO Record Script — RetroQuest × RetroForge

**33 lines · 420 words · 3:15 programme**
This is the clean read. Everything the voice needs is here; nothing else is.

---

## Direction

**The voice is dry, unhurried, and slightly amused.** Think a documentary narrator who genuinely
likes this stuff, not a trailer announcer. The game is a 1982 tribute made in 2026 and it knows it —
the delivery should have that same straight face.

**Specifics:**
- No rising inflection at the end of statements. Every line lands flat and stops.
- Numbers are the punchlines. "*Seventy-eight* monsters." "Twenty-*two* of them." Let them sit.
- Never push volume. The mix has room; the performance shouldn't fill it.
- **Act 4 (lines 16–23) drops half a step in energy and slows down.** That act is the descent, the
  music has stopped, and the voice should sound like someone lowering their tone in a large room.
- **Act 6 (lines 25–31) is staccato.** Short sentences, quick, almost a list. It is the deliberate
  opposite of Act 4, and the contrast is the point.

**Record two passes of every line** — one at the direction above, one 10% warmer. Act 5's line 24
gets three: the whole turn hangs on it.

## Booth spec

- Large-diaphragm condenser, cardioid, ~15cm with a pop filter slightly off-axis.
- 48kHz / 24-bit WAV. **One file per line, named `VO_S05.wav`, `VO_S06.wav`** — matching the shot
  IDs, not the line numbers, so the editor can drop them straight onto the timeline.
- Room tone: 30 seconds, recorded at the top of the session, delivered as `VO_ROOMTONE.wav`.
- No processing on delivery. No compression, no EQ, no noise reduction, no de-esser.
- Slate each take verbally, then leave a full second of clean air before the line starts.
